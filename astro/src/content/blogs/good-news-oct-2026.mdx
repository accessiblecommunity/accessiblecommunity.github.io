What happens when technology, community partnerships, and a commitment to inclusion come together?

Around the world, people are finding new ways to make everyday experiences more accessible. From visiting museums and traveling by train to using computers and accessing information, these developments create more opportunities for people with disabilities.

Accessibility progress does not always require a major breakthrough. Sometimes it begins with a new feature in a familiar device, a donation that makes technology more affordable, or a service that gives people another way to navigate their communities.

This October, we are highlighting eight positive developments from around the world. Each story demonstrates how practical changes can help reduce barriers and support greater independence and participation.

## 1\. Meta Donates 1,000 AI Glasses to Disability Organizations in Singapore

**Location: Singapore \| October 7, 2026**

Technology can provide new ways for people with disabilities to access information and navigate everyday activities.

Meta announced that it would donate 1,000 Ray-Ban Meta artificial intelligence (AI) glasses to four organizations in Singapore. The recipients include the Singapore Association of the Visually Handicapped, Guide Dogs Singapore, the Singapore Disability Sports Council, and Gardens by the Bay.

The glasses include features that can help people who are blind or have low vision read text, identify objects, and describe their surroundings.

Meta also announced a US\$30,000 grant to support accessibility training through the Singapore Association of the Visually Handicapped. The training will help people learn how to use the glasses safely and effectively.

The partnership brings together technology and organisations that understand the needs of their communities. Training is particularly important because people need more than access to a device. They also need to understand its features, limitations, and practical uses.

**Why it matters:** Assistive technology can provide additional options for independence and participation. Partnerships with disability organisations and investment in training can help ensure that new tools reach people who may benefit from them.

[Read Meta's announcement about the donation](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/10/meta-donates-1000-ai-glasses-to-singapores-disability-community/)

## 2\. An Italian Museum Uses AI Glasses to Make Art More Accessible

**Location: Milan, Italy \| October 23, 2026**

Visiting an art museum is an opportunity to explore history, creativity, and culture. However, traditional museum displays often depend heavily on visual information.

A project at Milan's Pinacoteca di Brera is offering visitors who are blind or have low vision another way to experience artworks.

The museum partnered with Meta and eyewear manufacturer EssilorLuxottica to introduce an experience called *Sensing Art: Discovering Masterpieces Beyond What Meets the Eye*.

Visitors can use Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses to explore eight artworks. When someone stands in front of a selected artwork, the glasses recognize it and provide spoken descriptions.

These descriptions include information about colors, shapes, composition, the artist, and the artwork's history.

Visitors can also ask Meta AI questions or use Be My Eyes to connect with volunteers for live visual assistance.

Importantly, people who are blind or have low vision helped develop and test the experience. The project also builds on the museum's existing DescriVedendo programme, which has provided audio descriptions for almost a decade.

The glasses are available free of charge to eligible visitors through a booking system.

Although the project currently operates at one museum, it demonstrates how cultural organizations can explore additional ways to make their collections accessible.

**Why it matters:** Accessibility should include cultural experiences, not just essential services. Giving visitors different ways to explore artwork can support greater independence and participation in museums and other public spaces.

[Read the report about the Italian museum's AI glasses project](https://www.accessibility.org.au/meta-ai-glasses-help-make-art-more-accessible-for-people-who-are-blind-or-with-low-vision/)

## 3\. NVDA Releases an Update with Screen Reader Improvements

**Location: Australia and international users \| October 30, 2026**

Screen readers are essential tools for many people who are blind or have low vision. They convert information displayed on a computer screen into speech or braille.

NV Access, an Australian not-for-profit organisation, released NVDA 2026.3, an update to its free and open-source screen reader.

The update includes improvements to performance, keyboard navigation, touchscreen accessibility, and braille support.

One improvement addresses delays when navigating applications such as Windows File Explorer. The update also fixes problems that could cause NVDA to freeze when another application stops responding.

Additional changes include new keyboard shortcuts, easier access to settings, and new touchscreen gestures. Braille users will also benefit from updated braille tables for several languages.

These improvements can make everyday computer activities more reliable, including browsing the internet, reading documents, and working with applications.

NVDA 2026.3 is available for Windows users.

**Why it matters:** Reliable screen reader access is essential for digital inclusion. Regular updates can help people access information, education, employment, and online services more effectively.

[Read about the NVDA 2026.3 update](https://www.accessibility.org.au/nvda-releases-new-update-with-screen-reader-improvements/)

## 4\. Bay Area Railway Trials Free Visual Assistance for Passengers

**Location: San Francisco Bay Area, United States \| October 20, 2026**

Navigating an unfamiliar railway station can be challenging, particularly when passengers need help locating signs, ticket machines, platforms, or entrances.

Bay Area Rapid Transit (BART) has introduced a trial that provides free visual assistance for passengers who are blind or have low vision.

The service uses the Aira Explorer smartphone app to connect passengers with trained visual interpreters. Through the phone's camera, interpreters can describe surroundings, read signs, and help people navigate stations.

The service is available around the clock, seven days a week, for BART-related journeys during the trial.

Passengers do not need to make an appointment or wait for a staff member to become available. They can connect with an interpreter when they need assistance.

The trial began on October 6 and is scheduled to continue until January 3, 2027\. BART will collect feedback from passengers to help determine whether the service should continue.

**Why it matters:** Accessible public transportation involves more than ramps and elevators. Passengers also need access to information and navigation support. On-demand visual assistance offers another way for people with visual disabilities to travel more independently.

[Read about BART's visual assistance trial](https://www.accessibility.org.au/us-railway-introduces-free-visual-assistance-for-passengers-who-are-blind-or-have-low-vision/)

## 5\. Orbit Player Adds Amazon Alexa for People Who Are Blind or Have Low Vision

**Location: International \| October 16, 2026**

Assistive technology is most useful when people can interact with it in ways that meet their access needs.

Orbit Research announced that Amazon Alexa is now available on its Orbit Player, a portable device designed to provide access to books and other digital content.

The addition allows users to interact with Alexa through voice commands or the device's physical buttons.

Users can ask questions, listen to news, play music and podcasts, and access services such as Audible and Kindle.

One important aspect of this development is that users do not have to rely on a touchscreen. The Alexa feature works with the Orbit Player's built-in screen reader and tactile controls.

The development also involved collaboration with Xavier's Resource Centre for the Visually Challenged at St. Xavier's College in Mumbai, India.

The update began rolling out to existing Orbit Player users on October 12\.

Orbit Research has clarified that Alexa is a third-party application and that it does not provide technical support for Alexa itself.

**Why it matters:** Accessible controls give people more ways to interact with technology. Supporting physical buttons and screen reader access can make digital services more usable for people who cannot easily use a touchscreen.

[Read about Alexa on the Orbit Player](https://www.accessibility.org.au/orbit-player-adds-amazon-alexa-for-people-who-are-blind-or-have-low-vision/)

## 6\. More Than 40 Laptops Donated to Support Digital Access in Australia

**Location: Western Australia \| October 9, 2026**

Having access to a computer is essential for many everyday activities. People use computers to apply for jobs, study, communicate, access services, and manage their personal responsibilities.

However, the cost of technology can prevent some people with disabilities from having their own devices.

Ngala, a Western Australian not-for-profit organization, donated more than 40 laptops to Centre for Accessibility Australia.

The laptops will be distributed through the organization's Accessible Tech for All programme. The programme provides refurbished computers to people with disabilities who may otherwise struggle to afford them.

The donation gives older devices another useful purpose while helping address the digital divide.

Access to a computer can make a significant difference for someone who needs assistive technology, accessible software, or reliable internet access to participate in education and employment.

The initiative also demonstrates how organisations can support digital inclusion by donating equipment they no longer need.

**Why it matters:** Digital accessibility requires access to devices, not just accessible websites and applications. Providing affordable computers can help people with disabilities participate in education, employment, and community life.

[Read about Ngala's laptop donation](https://www.accessibility.org.au/ngala-donates-more-than-40-laptops-to-support-digital-access-for-people-with-disability/)

## 7\. United Nations in the Philippines Highlights Disability Inclusion Throughout October

**Location: Philippines \| October 6, 2026**

Disability inclusion requires more than awareness. It involves recognizing the rights, leadership, and contributions of people with disabilities.

The United Nations in the Philippines and its partners launched a month-long celebration, UN Month 2026, focused on disability inclusion.

The campaign uses the theme *UNlimited Together* and highlights the importance of ensuring people with disabilities can fully participate in society. The program includes activities such as the UN Philippines Accessibility Challenge, the Voices of Inclusion digital campaign, and the Inclusive Futures Forum. The campaign also builds momentum toward the twentieth anniversary of the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, adopted in December 2006\.

The initiative brings together government partners, organizations of persons with disabilities, civil society, academic institutions, and private organizations.

By creating opportunities for different groups to work together, the campaign aims to increase awareness, encourage action, and strengthen partnerships.

**Why it matters:** Disability inclusion is a shared responsibility. Public campaigns can help amplify the voices of people with disabilities and encourage organizations to address the barriers that prevent equal participation.

[Read the United Nations announcement](https://philippines.un.org/en/324275-united-nations-launches-month-long-celebration-disability-inclusion)

## 8\. New Jersey Introduces Free Accessible Transportation Pilot

**Location: Camden and Gloucester Counties, New Jersey, United States \| October 28, 2026**

Transportation is closely connected to independence. It helps people reach work, education, medical appointments, and community activities.

NJ TRANSIT introduced the CG Hopper, a free, on-demand shared-ride transportation service for seniors and people with disabilities in Camden and Gloucester counties.

Via operates the service in partnership with Senior Citizens United Community Services and the two counties.

Passengers can use the service for trips within the designated area. It also provides connections to NJ TRANSIT services, giving riders access to a wider transportation network.

The pilot offers more flexible transportation options, including same-day rides and expanded service hours.

The program is planned as an 18-month pilot. Rutgers University's Alan M. Voorhees Transportation Center will help evaluate service data and outcomes to identify lessons for future transportation initiatives.

The service is available to residents aged 60 and older and people with disabilities who live in the participating counties.

**Why it matters:** Affordable and accessible transportation can reduce barriers to employment, healthcare, and community participation. Flexible services can provide additional options for people who cannot always rely on traditional public transportation.

[Read NJ TRANSIT's announcement about the CG Hopper](https://www.njtransit.com/press-releases/nj-transit-introduces-free-pilot-microtransit-service-seniors-and-people)

## Looking Ahead: Small Changes Can Create Meaningful Progress

This month's roundup highlights eight developments that show accessibility can take many forms.

It can mean making art available through audio descriptions, improving screen reader performance, providing free transportation, or helping someone obtain a computer.

It can also mean recognising that people with disabilities should have opportunities to participate in cultural activities, access information, travel independently, and use technology on their own terms.

These developments are encouraging, but there is still work to do. New services need to reach the people who need them. Technology needs to be tested with disabled users. Organisations need to keep listening, learning, and improving.

Most importantly, people with disabilities should be involved in shaping the solutions that affect their lives.
